Brocco vs Zapier.

Zapier runs deterministic steps. Brocco runs agents that reason. They solve different problems. Here's exactly when to reach for which.

The core difference

A Zapier zap is a recipe: when X, do Y, then Z. Beautiful when the steps are knowable up front. Brittle when they aren't.

A Brocco agent is a reasoner: given a goal and a tool registry, it picks which tool to call next based on what it sees. It can branch, retry, adapt to weird inputs, and end early when the job is done.

The right tool depends on whether your workflow is "if-this-then-that" or "figure out what to do next."

When to use which

Reach for Zapier when…

  • The steps are knowable: form fills → CRM, Calendly → Slack, Stripe charge → invoice email.
  • Volume is high, decisions are zero: you process 10,000 of the same event and just need glue.
  • You want a 7,000-app library with no coding.
  • The tradeoff: any time the input shape changes, the zap breaks loudly.

Reach for Brocco when…

  • The next step depends on what was just read: research, synthesis, drafting, qualification.
  • The input is messy: emails, PDFs, scraped HTML, unstructured forms, voice transcripts.
  • You want one entity that owns a workflow end-to-end, not a 30-step chain.
  • The tradeoff: you pay for tokens. Caching brings it under most Zapier task pricing in practice.

The real answer is both. Zapier triggers Brocco. Brocco emits a Slack message that fires another zap. Use the right tool for each layer.
