The 2026 agentic AI platform landscape

Twenty platforms surveyed. Three white spaces nobody owns. One moat that everyone underestimated until 2026.

CursorDevinManusSierra DecagonLindyRelevanceCrewAI LangGraphOperatorMarinerVapi + 8 more

We surveyed 20 commercial agentic AI platforms shipping in production this quarter. Pricing, positioning, hero copy, killer feature, weakness. Three patterns came out of it that you cannot unsee once you've seen them.

1. The agent itself is a commodity

Look at the underlying models. Cursor, Windsurf, Devin, Claude Code, Replit Agent, Manus, Lindy, Relevance. Every single one of them runs on the same four model families: Claude, GPT, Gemini, and a long tail of open weights. The "agent" everyone is selling is, mechanically, a tool-use loop wrapped around a state-of-the-art frontier model.

The wrappers vary in polish. The substrate doesn't.

You can see it in the pricing too. Sonnet 4.6 ($3 in / $15 out per million tokens) and Opus 4.7 ($5 / $25) have set the floor. Gemini 3 Pro and Manus 1.6 Lite are within striking distance. So when Cursor charges $20/mo, Devin charges $20/mo, Claude Pro charges $20/mo, and ChatGPT Pro charges $20/mo, that's not a coincidence. It's the price of frontier intelligence routed through someone's UX. The UX matters. But it's not a moat.

2. The pricing is converging

Here's the consensus ladder we found across pure-play AI workspaces in 2026:

Two notable mutations from the SaaS playbook:

  • Free tier, not free trial. Every winner in 2026 ships free-forever with hard limits. "14-day free trial" reads as 2019.
  • Outcome and credit pricing are eating subscriptions. Sierra (per-resolution), Decagon (per-conversation), Cursor (credits), Replit (effort-based), Manus (credits), Devin (ACUs). The flat-monthly era is bleeding out.

3. Three white spaces nobody owns yet

Vertical-specific autonomous agents that own a P&L line

Everyone sells horizontal "build any agent." Nobody owns "the agent that runs your wholesaling acquisition pipeline" or "the agent that runs your DTC paid-social refresh cycle."

Sierra and Decagon proved verticalizing CX works at $150M ARR. But only one vertical (support). The other 50 verticals are wide open. You don't sell agents. You sell the result of an agent wired into a specific industry's data and workflows.

Outcome-priced SMB

Sierra invented outcome-based pricing but priced it for F500 ($150K floor). Lindy and Relevance are SMB but flat-subscription. Nobody owns "$0 if it doesn't close, $X if it does" for SMB lead-gen, real-estate dispo, e-commerce CRO.

The wired-in proprietary-data agent

Every horizontal platform above is a thin orchestration layer over the same four model families. What nobody owns is "agents wired into private operational data and battle-tested workflows you can't get anywhere else."

What this means for you

If you're building an agentic platform, the message is dead clear: don't sell the agent. Sell the workflow. The agent is the cheap part. What you do for a customer that nobody else can. That's worth subscribing to.

If you're a buyer, the lesson inverts: don't pay extra for orchestration alone. Pay for the integrations and the SOPs the platform brings to your specific business. The runtime is roughly the same everywhere.

Brocco took this brief literally. Our runtime, called Charter, is open in spirit. The agent is a commodity. Your business isn't. We made the runtime cheap and the integrations yours. That's the bet.
