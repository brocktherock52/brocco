Why brocco exists

I have a graveyard of unfinished projects. UGC pipelines, real-estate wholesaling automation, a Polymarket bot, a Series 7 prep funnel, a Detroit fix-flip dispo pack. None of them shipped to a paying customer last quarter, all of them needed AI agents, and I was bouncing between five tabs to do anything useful with one.

The pattern was always the same. Research the competitor in tab A. Draft the outreach in tab B. Plan the launch in tab C. Tab D for the screenshot, tab E for the spreadsheet. The agents were good. The orchestration was me.

Brocco is the dashboard I built to make myself the orchestrator instead of the operator. Pick three agents, type one prompt, watch them fan out in parallel. The agent is the cheap part; the workflow is the moat. We just made the workflow yours.

Operating principles

  • Ship cadence over polish cadence. Ten versions in a week beats one polished version in a month. The changelog shows the trail.
  • BYOK by default. Your key, your tokens, your data. We never see your prompts on the free tier.
  • No VC, no growth-hacking funnels. Pricing is what the API costs plus a margin. No upsell traps, no hidden tiers, one-click cancel.
  • Honest about tradeoffs. If brocco is the wrong tool for your job (Cursor for IDE work, Claude Code for terminal-bound dev) we tell you on the comparison pages.
  • Build in public. Stripe activation status, subscriber count, ship log: all public.

The stack, end-to-end

Runtime: charter (open Python package, the Anthropic tool-use loop with prompt caching, multi-agent orchestration, sandboxed tool registry). App: vanilla JS in the browser, no React, no framework, custom WebGL hero shader. Hosting: Vercel for static + Edge functions; Hetzner for long-running agent workers. Billing: Stripe. Search: Tavily. Models: Anthropic Claude (default), or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (Ollama for local, vLLM, OpenRouter, etc).

The architecture is documented in the quickstart and the runtime is on GitHub for anyone to read or self-host.
